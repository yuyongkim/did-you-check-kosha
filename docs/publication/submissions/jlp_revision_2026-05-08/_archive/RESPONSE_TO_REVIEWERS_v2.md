### Comment R3.1: K-voting design rationale — why this choice and why those layers

> *"The author should explain why K-voting is chosen and why it needs those layers."*

**Author response:** We thank the reviewer; this comment prompted §5.4, a new dedicated subsection titled *"Why three paths and a 1% tolerance? — K-voting design rationale"*. The subsection justifies three orthogonal design decisions explicitly. **(i) Number of paths.** Two paths can detect inconsistency but cannot resolve it; three paths give majority-vote semantics with a single comparison, which captures the typical numerical-engineering failure mode (one path drifting from two that agree). Five or more paths give marginal coverage gain at proportionally higher implementation cost and synchronisation overhead; the choice is consistent with the original 3-version baseline studied in [11]. **(ii) Tolerance.** The 1% threshold is calibrated against the tightest acceptance tolerance in the golden dataset (±1% for critical cases per §6.1); a tighter threshold would generate false-positive consensus failures arising from legitimate floating-point rounding differences between paths, and a looser threshold would mask real coding errors. **(iii) Scope of independence.** Full N-version programming requires independently authored implementations to defeat correlated bugs; we do not claim that level of independence — the three paths share the same author and the same standard-citation tables. We make this limitation explicit so reviewers can correctly position the layer: K-voting here protects primarily against numerical-precision and ordering bugs, **not** against systematic conceptual errors in standard interpretation. We additionally note in §6.6 (Layer-B ablation, Table 7b) that K-voting is a **verification-quality** layer that does not, by itself, contribute new detections — its role is to suppress false-positive numerical-precision artefacts. This positioning addresses the reviewer's "why it needs those layers" question by making each layer's responsibility distinct and testable.

Editor and Reviewer comments:  

Reviewer #1: Overall the concept is interesting and applicable in industry and worthy of investigation. However, I feel the work needs to be overhauled to demonstrate that this is an effective tool compared to other methods.

Intro secions briefly discuss other AI methods for detecting gaps. I would generally like to see a comparison against the state of the art, but the tool presented here left me wondering how this method compares against process experts that do this assessment now. I think this is worth mentioning, otherwise the findings are presented in a vacuum and I don't really know how effective the model is.

There's no data presented to support the conclusions and limited discussion of the results and the method's effectiveness. In your RQ1 there's a claim that the model predicts 25 cross discipline scenarios, but there are no further discussions into the details: what was predicted, why did this model find the scenarios, are they significant findings.

Conclusions only show that errors can be detected by RQ1, is the model better at predicting particular problems (civil, electrical, etc) are there strengths and weaknesses

No Figures and minimal data to support conclusions. RQ2 for example lists three gaps detected, but there's no further discussion, was this expected, is it significant, were any gaps missed? There really needs to be a basis for comparison, otherwise it is difficilt for me to accept the results.

Relies on synthetic data that could skew the results. Did you find exactly what you know the model would find? This is why I feel a comparison to another methodology is needed.

Reviewer #2: This manuscript presents a novel and relevant contribution to process safety by introducing the concept of the jurisdiction compliance gap and proposing a Regulatory RAG-based verification layer to bridge engineering calculations and regulatory obligations. The topic is highly aligned with modern challenges in digitalisation, PSM compliance, and multi-standard environments (e.g., ASME/API vs national regulations such as KOSHA). The integration of cross-discipline validation and regulatory retrieval is particularly promising and could have strong industrial impact.
However, several areas require strengthening before the work is suitable for publication. For details please see the attached file.

Reviewer #3: The topic of the manuscript is very interesting, and the author addresses a known blind spot between engineering correctness and regulatory compliance. That's rare and valuable. However, the manuscript requires a major revision before it can be considered suitable for publication. The manuscript is written in a more technical and professional style than a typical scientific article. The author should carefully review the manuscript formatting guidelines.

The author should explain why K-voting is chosen and why it needs those layers.
